Verdict

Detection is not the problem; calibration is. On 42 seeded defects across 18 defective artifacts, every approach except stop-early catches ≥99% with a blocking attack, and every approach sends 100% of defective artifacts to ATTACKS. The approaches separate almost entirely on clean artifacts: how often they invent a blocker and fail something that should pass.

  • Best overall: single-strong — one strong-tier (sonnet) agent with the full 15-lens rubric plus free attack. bAcc 92% (CI 83–100), TNR 83%, 1.0 bogus blockers/run, 5.1k tokens, 11 s, $0.023 list. Cheapest on tokens of any non-truncating approach.
  • The elephant-gold scheme loses. 15 lens specialists + free generalist + haiku compiler: 10.9 bogus blockers/run, TNR 13%, 96k tokens, 17 calls. With a sonnet compiler (elephant-gold-sc) TNR recovers to 63% — still worse than one sonnet call, at 18× the tokens and 4× the list $. More perspectives produce more plausible-sounding blockers; a compiler only partly filters them, and the same recall@3 (82%) is reached by one call.
  • The incumbent counsel-5x2 is the worst gate tested. TNR 3% (it blocks nearly every clean artifact), 162k tokens, 11 calls, 80 s critical path — 31× the tokens and 7× the latency of single-strong for no detection gain. Deliberation rounds amplify objections; nothing in the protocol rewards clearing.
  • The verdict seat's model tier dominates. Every haiku-verdict approach lands at TNR 10–20% regardless of topology (single rubric, self-consistency, clustered panel, triage, elephant-gold). Swapping only the compiler to sonnet moves clustered-panel from 10% → 63% TNR. A prompt-only calibration clause on haiku (rubric-calibrated) moves it just 17% → 20%.
  • Stop-early is a cheap verdict, not a review. 5.2k tokens, 1 call, 7 s, TNR 40%, TPR 100% — but only 31% of defects reach the author, so the fix loop needs extra rounds.
  • The cascade did not cascade. Haiku returned a confident CLEAR in 2/72 runs, so 97% of runs paid for both tiers ($0.042, 2× single-strong) and ended slightly less calibrated (TNR 77%). A cascade only pays if the cheap tier can clear; here it can't.

Method

  • Corpus. 36 realistic artifacts (12 each: plan, merge diff, deploy brief) written by subagents blind to the approaches. 18 carry 42 seeded defects across 15 lenses with evidence quotes; 18 are clean controls (12 of them added after the first pass showed calibration was the signal). Decoys (things that look wrong but are fine) are planted in both. check-corpus.ts verifies schema, evidence-in-artifact and no signpost words.
  • Runs. 12 approaches × 36 cases × 2 reps = 864 runs via claude -p with all ambient context stripped. Verdict is recomputed from the contract (ATTACKS iff any blocker/major attack), not trusted from the model's own field.
  • Judge. Opus, one call per case-rep, sees all 12 submissions anonymized and shuffled, labels every attack: defect id | decoy | extra (real, unkeyed) | nit | wrong | dup. Reliability: 17/18 audited matches precise; full re-judge test-retest over 7,010 attacks = 99.7% agreement on defect-catch sets, 93% exact-label agreement (drift confined to nit/extra/dup boundaries).
  • Disputed controls. A clean case is excluded from headline TNR when, in either judging pass, ≥2/3 of approaches raised a blocking attack the judge labelled a real problem ("extra"). This flags plan-03 (rollback drops unsubscribes), plan-09 (erasure not applied to the v2 table during backfill) and deploy-09 (QA claims a pilot passed that the rollout schedules later) — plausible authoring misses. Raw-key TNR is shown in grey; the ranking does not change.
  • Stats. Balanced accuracy = (TPR+TNR)/2 with case-level bootstrap 95% CIs. Cost = Claude list price reported by the CLI (a Max seat in practice). Critical path = sum of each stage's slowest call, assuming full parallel fan-out.

Threats to validity

  • Too easy to detect. LLM-authored defects saturate recall; the bench cannot separate approaches on detection. A hard tier (subtle, multi-hop, human-authored defects) is needed before claiming fan-out has no recall value.
  • Small clean n. 15 undisputed clean controls × 2 reps; CIs on TNR are wide (single-strong vs cascade vs the -sc panels overlap). The haiku-vs-sonnet verdict-seat gap is far outside the noise; finer orderings are not.
  • Claude-only, two tiers. No opus gate, no cross-vendor panel. Cross-model diversity is the strongest untested argument for panels.
  • Counsel emulated. No tools, research or repo access; real /counsel may behave differently. Its round structure is reproduced faithfully.
  • Contract-derived verdict. Any major attack blocks. Approaches that hedge with "major" are punished; that is the gate contract, but a softer contract would shift TNR.
  • Judge is opus and same-vendor. Measured stable, not proven correct; disputed controls show the key itself can be wrong.
  • Domain overlap. plan-11 and deploy-09 share a domain (library SMS hold alerts).
  • Interruption. The run paused on a seat session limit and resumed; all 864 runs and 72 judgings completed with 2 infra faults (unparseable JSON), both re-run.
